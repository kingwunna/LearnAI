TOPIC 1.1: MEASURING LENGTH & VOLUME
Question 1 [Paper 2 / Paper 6 Common Diagram Question] [Total: 4 marks]
A student uses a measuring cylinder containing water to measure the volume of a small, irregularly shaped stone.
Fig. 1.1: Measuring volume of an irregular solid by water displacement.
(a) State the initial volume $V_1$ of the water in Diagram 1.1 and the final volume $V_2$ in Diagram 1.2. [2]
(b) Calculate the volume $V$ of the stone. [1]
(c) State two precautions taken when taking reading $V_1$ from the measuring cylinder to ensure accuracy. [1]
Mark Scheme & Model Answers:
  • (a) $V_1 = 30 ext{ cm}^3$, $V_2 = 42 ext{ cm}^3$ [2 marks]
  • (b) $ ext{Volume} = V_2 - V_1 = 42 - 30 = 12 ext{ cm}^3$ [1 mark]
  • (c) Any two from:
    • View scale at eye level / line of sight perpendicular to scale (to avoid parallax error).
    • Read at the bottom of the liquid meniscus.
    • Place measuring cylinder on a flat/horizontal bench.
    [1 mark]
Question 2 [Micrometer Screw Gauge Reading] [Total: 3 marks]
Fig. 1.2 shows the enlarged scale of a micrometer screw gauge being used to measure the diameter of a thin copper wire.
Fig. 1.2: Micrometer scale reading.
(a) Record the reading shown on the main sleeve scale. [1]
(b) Record the reading shown on the rotating thimble scale. [1]

TOPIC 1.2: DENSITY DETERMINATION & FLOATING
Question 3 [Paper 6 (0625_m23_qp_62) Density of Marbles Experiment] [Total: 6 marks]
A student determines the density of the glass from which marbles are made using a top-pan balance and a measuring cylinder.
Fig. 1.3: Experimental measurements to determine the density of glass marbles.
(a) Calculate the average mass $m$ of one marble. [2]
(b) Calculate the average volume $V$ of one marble using $V_1 = 26 ext{ cm}^3$ and $V_2 = 38 ext{ cm}^3$. [2]
(c) Calculate the density $ ho$ of the glass, giving the correct unit. [2]
Mark Scheme & Model Answers:
  • (a) Mass of 5 marbles $= M_5 - M_0 = 64.3 - 30.8 = 33.5 ext{ g}$.
    Average mass of one marble $m = 33.5 / 5 = \mathbf{6.7 ext{ g}}$ [2 marks]
  • (b) Total volume of 5 marbles $= V_2 - V_1 = 38 - 26 = 12 ext{ cm}^3$.
    Average volume of one marble $V = 12 / 5 = \mathbf{2.4 ext{ cm}^3}$ [2 marks]
  • (c) $ ho = rac{m}{V} = rac{6.7 ext{ g}}{2.4 ext{ cm}^3} = \mathbf{2.79 ext{ g/cm}^3}$ (or $2.8 ext{ g/cm}^3$ / $2800 ext{ kg/m}^3$) [2 marks]
Question 4 [Immiscible Liquids & Floating/Sinking] [Total: 4 marks]
Fig. 1.4 shows a tall glass jar containing three immiscible liquids (olive oil, water, and dense syrup) and two solid objects (a wooden block and a steel bolt).
Fig. 1.4: Density layers of liquids and floating/sinking objects.
(a) Explain why olive oil forms a layer on top of the water. [1]
(b) A plastic bead of density $1.15 ext{ g/cm}^3$ is dropped into the jar. State where the bead comes to rest. Explain your answer. [2]
(c) State the rule that determines whether an object floats or sinks in a liquid. [1]
Mark Scheme & Model Answers:
  • (a) Olive oil has a lower density ($0.90 ext{ g/cm}^3$) than water ($1.00 ext{ g/cm}^3$) and is immiscible. [1 mark]
  • (b) The bead comes to rest at the boundary between the water and the syrup (sinks through water, floats on syrup) because its density ($1.15 ext{ g/cm}^3$) is greater than water ($1.00 ext{ g/cm}^3$) but less than syrup ($1.40 ext{ g/cm}^3$). [2 marks]
  • (c) An object floats if its overall density is less than the liquid's density; it sinks if its density is greater. [1 mark]
TOPIC 1.3: MEASURING TIME & PENDULUM OSCILLATIONS
Question 5 [Paper 6 (0625_w23_qp_62 & 0625_w24_qp_61) Simple Pendulum] [Total: 5 marks]
A student investigates how the period $T$ of a simple pendulum depends on its length $d$.
Fig. 1.5: Pendulum apparatus and digital stopwatch reading.
(a) Read the stopwatch in Fig. 1.5 and state the time $t$ for 10 complete oscillations. [1]
(b) Calculate the period $T$ of the pendulum (the time for 1 complete oscillation). [1]
(c) Calculate $T^2$. [1]
(d) Explain why measuring the time for 10 oscillations improves the accuracy of period $T$ compared to measuring a single oscillation. [1]
(e) Describe one technique to ensure that length $d$ is measured accurately. [1]
Mark Scheme & Model Answers:
  • (a) Time $t = \mathbf{14.20 ext{ s}}$ (or $14.2 ext{ s}$) [1 mark]
  • (b) Period $T = rac{t}{10} = rac{14.20}{10} = \mathbf{1.42 ext{ s}}$ [1 mark]
  • (c) $T^2 = (1.42)^2 = \mathbf{2.02 ext{ s}^2}$ [1 mark]
  • (d) Reduces the effect / percentage uncertainty of human reaction time errors (as the reaction error is spread over 10 oscillations). [1 mark]
  • (e) Any one valid technique:
    • Use a set-square horizontally from the bottom of the clamp to the ruler.
    • Measure to the centre of the spherical bob (or measure to top and bottom and average).
    • Hold the metre rule close and parallel to the pendulum string.
    [1 mark]
CHAPTER 1 SUMMARY CHEATSHEET FOR EXAMS
Key Formulas to Memorise:
  • $ ext{Density } ( ho) = rac{ ext{Mass } (m)}{ ext{Volume } (V)}$ (Units: $ ext{g/cm}^3$ or $ ext{kg/m}^3$)
  • $ ext{Conversion: } 1 ext{ g/cm}^3 = 1000 ext{ kg/m}^3$
  • $ ext{Period of Pendulum } (T) = rac{ ext{Total time for } N ext{ oscillations } (t)}{N}$
  • $ ext{Volume of Cuboid} = l imes w imes h \quad | \quad ext{Volume of Cylinder} = \pi r^2 h$
