Sphere in a Cylinder – Practice

Two questions similar to the Cambridge-style displaced-water problem.

Problem 1

A cylinder contains water. A solid metal sphere is touching the base of the cylinder.

Half of the sphere is in the water.

The radius of the cylinder is 8 cm and the radius of the sphere is 4 cm.

The sphere is removed from the cylinder and the level of the water decreases by h cm.

Calculate h. Give your answer exactly and as a decimal. [3]

Volume of a sphere: V = 4/3 × πr³
Only half the sphere is underwater, so the displaced volume is ½ × sphere volume. Set this equal to π(8²)h.

Half-sphere volume = ½ × 4/3 × π × 4³ = 128π/3.

Decrease in cylinder water volume = π × 8² × h = 64πh.

So 64πh = 128π/3.

h = 128π/3 ÷ 64π = 2/3 cm = 0.67 cm (to 2 d.p.).

Problem 2

A cylindrical tank contains water. A solid sphere rests on the base.

Exactly half of the sphere is below the water surface.

The radius of the cylinder is 15 cm and the radius of the sphere is 6 cm.

The sphere is taken out. The water level falls by x cm.

Calculate x. Give your answer exactly and as a decimal. [3]

Volume of a sphere: V = 4/3 × πr³
Find the volume of the submerged half of the sphere, then divide by the cylinder's cross-sectional area π(15²).

Half-sphere volume = ½ × 4/3 × π × 6³ = 144π.

Decrease in cylinder water volume = π × 15² × x = 225πx.

225πx = 144π.

x = 144/225 = 16/25 = 0.64 cm.

Tip: Volume displaced by the submerged part of the sphere = decrease in water volume after the sphere is removed.
