1.1 Measuring Length & Volume

Choose the correct instrument, measure accurately, and use displacement for irregular objects.

Paper 2 + Paper 6

Choosing the correct instrument

InstrumentSmallest division / precisionTypical use
Metre rule / ruler1 mm (0.1 cm)General lengths up to about 1 m
Vernier calipers0.1 mm (0.01 cm)Internal/external diameters and depth
Micrometer screw gauge0.01 mm (0.001 cm)Thin wire, paper or glass thickness
Measuring cylinderRead scale carefullyLiquid volume and irregular solid volume
Thin objects

Measure many, then divide

If one sheet or one wire turn is too thin to measure accurately, measure a large number together.

average thickness = total thickness ÷ N
Exam phrase: wind wire turns closely with no gaps or overlaps.
Regular solids

Volume formulae

  • Cuboid: V = l × w × h
  • Cube: V = s³
  • Cylinder: V = πr²h
  • Sphere: V = 4/3 πr³
Irregular solids

Water displacement

  1. Record initial water volume V₁.
  2. Fully submerge the object using thin thread.
  3. Record final volume V₂.
  4. Object volume = V₂ − V₁.
Parallax: view the scale at eye level / at right angles. For a normal liquid in a measuring cylinder, read the bottom of the meniscus.
Zero error: check calipers or micrometer reads zero before measurement and correct if needed.
Ruler alignment: keep the ruler adjacent and parallel to the object.

1.2 Density

Connect mass, volume, floating and sinking.

Core equation
Equation

Density

ρ = m / V

SI unit: kg/m³
Alternative: g/cm³

1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³   •   1 cm³ = 1 mL
Regular solid

Experimental method

  1. Measure mass using a balance.
  2. Measure dimensions.
  3. Calculate volume.
  4. Use ρ = m/V.
Irregular solid

Experimental method

  1. Measure the dry object's mass first.
  2. Find volume by displacement.
  3. Use ρ = m/V.
Trap: water clinging to the object can increase the measured mass.

Density of a liquid

Measure empty cylinder mass m₁, then cylinder + liquid mass m₂. The liquid mass is m₂ − m₁. Measure liquid volume V, then calculate density.

Floating & sinking

An object floats if its density is less than the liquid density. It sinks if its density is greater than the liquid density.

Immiscible liquids

Liquids that do not mix form layers. The lowest density liquid is on top; the highest density liquid is at the bottom.

1.3 Measuring Time

Measure oscillations more accurately and understand period and frequency.

Paper 6 favourite

Period & frequency

Period T: time for one complete oscillation.

T = t / N

Frequency f: oscillations per second.

f = 1 / T

Accurate pendulum timing

  1. Use a fiducial mark at the centre of swing.
  2. Release from a small angle.
  3. Time 10 or 20 complete oscillations.
  4. Divide total time by number of oscillations.
  5. Repeat and average.

Why time many oscillations?

Human reaction time becomes a smaller percentage of the total measured time, so the calculated period is more accurate.

Light gates / data loggers remove human reaction-time error for fast events.

5 exam-style sets

Q1 — Water displacement

Irregular stone volume and accurate cylinder reading.

(a) State V₁ and V₂. (b) Calculate the stone volume. (c) State two reading precautions.
V₁ = 30 cm³, V₂ = 42 cm³. Stone volume = 42 − 30 = 12 cm³. Read at eye level / perpendicular to the scale, read the bottom of the meniscus, and keep the cylinder on a level surface.

Q2 — Micrometer screw gauge

Read main scale + thimble scale.

(a) Main sleeve reading? (b) Thimble reading? (c) Total wire diameter?
Main scale = 2.50 mm. Thimble = 38 × 0.01 mm = 0.38 mm. Total = 2.88 mm.

Q3 — Density of glass marbles

Use balance and water displacement data.

Find average mass and volume of one marble, then calculate the glass density.
Mass of 5 marbles = 64.3 − 30.8 = 33.5 g → one marble = 6.7 g. Volume of 5 marbles = 38 − 26 = 12 cm³ → one marble = 2.4 cm³. Density = 6.7 ÷ 2.4 = 2.79 g/cm³ (≈ 2.8 g/cm³).

Q4 — Immiscible liquids

Predict layers using density.

Where does a bead of density 1.15 g/cm³ come to rest? Explain.
At the boundary between water and syrup. Its density is greater than water, so it sinks through water, but less than syrup, so it floats on syrup.

Q5 — Simple pendulum

Read stopwatch and calculate period.

Find t for 10 oscillations, period T and T². Why is timing 10 swings better?
t = 14.20 s. T = 14.20 ÷ 10 = 1.42 s. T² = 2.02 s². Timing many oscillations reduces the percentage effect of human reaction-time error.
